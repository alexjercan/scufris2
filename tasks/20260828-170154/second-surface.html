The Second Surface

Scufris answers on one screen, on one machine. This asks what it costs to answer on a laptop across the house and a phone in your pocket. The finding: the one-user law is not what stands in the way, and neither is one host — the rig keeps the agent. Two other clauses bundled into L1 are, and they cost wildly different amounts. Nothing here is built until v0.5.0 ships.

00

Laws

Bounds everything below

L1 of the inversion design says: one of everything, on one machine — one pi --mode rpc, one scufris-desktop, one person, one host, no tenancy, no account, no authentication, no network listener, no second device. It has been the most useful sentence in the project, because it answers questions instead of code.

It is also five claims in a trench coat. This design keeps four of them — one of those on a new footing — and replaces exactly one.

L1 — One person, one conversation, one host (narrowed)

One pi --mode rpc. One session directory. One transcript. One person. One host: the rig runs the agent, and only the rig ever does. No tenancy, no accounts, no per-user isolation, no second conversation running beside the first.

This is the part that was doing the work and it is untouched. Every "what if two people..." question is still answered here rather than in code, and so is every "what if two agents".

Exactly one clause leaves: one surface. "One host" was two claims wearing one name — the machine that runs Scufris, and the only device involved at all. The first stays a law, and it is the one that matters: everything authoritative stays on the rig. The second was never the same claim; it was only convenient to state alongside it. A surface is a client, and a client was never the host.

L5 — Scufris does not know where you are

"Scufris should not even realize we call it from a different machine. It should just be: I am Scufris, I am running on NixOS, I respond to whatever input I get." — Alex, on revision 1

That is the law, and it is stronger than a security argument. Nothing in the service distinguishes a local client from a remote one, because nothing in the service can, and nothing ever gets to ask. No remote flag on a client. No local-or-remote branch. No capability keyed to where a connection sits. A surface is a surface.

This is also the tripwire: if a future design needs to know whether a client is remote, L5 is being broken, and the thing to fix is the design rather than the law. Presence (L6) is the first test of it and passes — it follows what you last touched, which is blind to where you touched it from.

The security clause falls out rather than being argued for. The service keeps binding a mode 0600 Unix socket in the runtime directory and keeps authenticating nothing, because anything that reaches that socket is already a process running as you on the host. Every surface belongs to the same person, and the transport authenticates before the protocol sees a byte.

Scufris never opens a TCP listener. Not behind a password, not on localhost, not "just for testing". Whatever crosses a network is WireGuard's problem, or Tailscale's, or ssh's — all three of which do it better than we would, and none of which we have to test.

This is the law that keeps native/scufris-service/src/server.rs:5 literally true after the change: there is nothing to authenticate, and anything that can open the socket can already act as them. That sentence is the security model. A design that falsifies it owes a replacement, and this one does not falsify it.

L6 — Any number watch, exactly one attends

State and transcript go to every connected surface. Speech, widget commands and window raises go to exactly one: the surface you are looking at. That surface holds presence.

Presence is not a mode and not a setting. It is inferred from what you just did, the way the debug lease is inferred from a connection being open. There is no verb to get stuck between.

L2 — Nothing is migrated (kept, and made explicit)

L2 says no backwards compatibility in any direction: v3 replaced v2 rather than coexisting. That is affordable because one nixos-rebuild moves every part of Scufris at once, and a phone app is the first component that cannot ride along.

"It is what it is - keep this Law. If I update the host I better be updating the app. I imagine Scufris pinging me: hey, update the phone app when you can so we can keep talking." — Alex, on revision 1

Settled: L2 stays whole. The coupling stops being a hidden assumption and becomes a stated contract, and the cost is paid by Scufris telling you rather than by an app that silently half works. That fits what Scufris is already meant to do - the machine may initiate substantive contact, and "your host moved ahead of your phone" is exactly the kind of thing worth being told unasked.

One rule falls out of it, and it is sharp: the version handshake is the one part of the protocol that is frozen forever. hello, welcome, and the version refusal must keep working across every mismatch there will ever be, because they are the only thing that still runs when nothing else does. Freeze their shape now. Everything above them stays free to change, which is the whole point of L2.

Get that backwards and the notice you are counting on is the first thing that breaks. See 07.

01

One user is not what breaks

Thesis

"what if we add an integration for a Phone app? would that break our only one user rule? like I am thinking it could be just a new surface, basically just the pill as a phone app" — Alex, 2026-08-28

No. A phone, a laptop and the desktop, all operated by you, is still one person, one conversation, one session, one agent. Nothing about tenancy, accounts or isolation comes into it. The multi-user question is not on the table and this design does not put it there.

Take L1 apart clause by clause and the real cost becomes visible. Three of the five are untouched. One is cheap. One is the whole job.

"One host" is on the untouched side, and that is worth being exact about. The rig runs the agent, the session and the service, and it stays the only thing that ever does. What a second surface adds is a client, not a second Scufris. Nothing authoritative leaves the machine it is on today.

Clause of L1 Under a second surface Cost
One person Untouched. Still one of you. None
One agent Untouched. Surfaces are clients; the agent is one child. None
One conversation Untouched, and reinforced — that is the point of connecting a second surface at all. None
One host Untouched. The rig runs the agent, the session and the service, and stays the only thing that does. A surface is a client. None
No authentication Survives only if the transport does the authenticating. See L5. Zero code, one deployment decision
One surface Breaks. And it does not break gracefully — see 02. The whole job

So the design question is not "how do we support more than one user". It is: the service pushes to "every frontend" as though that were a synonym for "the screen". With more than one, it needs a word for the one you are looking at.

02

What the code already does

Audit, not speculation

Half of this is already built. The service is not a single-client server that would need rewriting; it is a fan-out server with one deliberate eviction in it.

Mechanism Today Right with N surfaces?
State pushes push_frontends loops every frontend (service.rs:131) Yes, as written
Transcript Same loop; 200-entry ring replayed on connect Yes. The ring is already the sync mechanism
Your own words Come off Pi's event stream at MessageEnd (service.rs:426), not off the submit path Yes — typing on the phone already shows on the laptop, free
Slow or dead client Bounded outbox of 256, then dropped (drop_client) Yes. A phone on a bad train is already handled
Concurrent submit Second one is delivered as a steer, not refused (service.rs:717) Yes, and it is the behaviour you want
Control clients Already unlimited — "that is just a person in a terminal" Yes
Second frontend Evicted by register (service.rs:518) No. And worse than no — see below
Speech Broadcast (service.rs:601) No. Two devices, one paragraph, out of phase
Widget commands Broadcast (service.rs:609) No. Two answers for one command id
Widget catalog Last announcement wins, stored and replayed (service.rs:673) No. Tools would flap by surface
Raise the window Broadcast (service.rs:656) No. Right on the laptop, noise in your pocket

The eviction does not fail closed — it livelocks

register removes the previous client in the same role. That drops its outbox, which ends its writer thread, which shuts the socket down. And link.rs reconnects with bounded backoff, because surviving a service restart is its job.

So two companions do not produce an error. A connects. B connects and evicts A. A reconnects and evicts B. B reconnects and evicts A. Forever, at roughly the 5-second ceiling of MAX_BACKOFF, with both conversation windows clearing on every cycle.

This is worth knowing before anyone tries the "just point the laptop at it" experiment and concludes the socket forward is broken. It is not. The eviction is.

03

The tunnel, not the listener

Zero code

Behind the service socket is an agent that runs commands, spawns tmux workers and writes your filesystem. An unauthenticated submit from the network is remote code execution with a friendly tone. This is the part of the design where being conservative is not timidity, it is arithmetic.

So the service does not learn about networks. The socket goes over a tunnel the operating system already authenticates, and OpenSSH forwards Unix sockets directly: 

# on the laptop, over WireGuard or Tailscale to the host
ssh -N -L "$HOME/.scufris-remote/service.sock":/run/user/1000/scufris/service.sock den

# then the companion, unchanged, pointed at the forwarded socket
SCUFRIS_RUNTIME_DIR="$HOME/.scufris-remote" scufris-desktop

Staging already built this

SCUFRIS_RUNTIME_DIR names the directory outright, and the service, the companion, scufris-ctl and the agent extension all resolve it the same way, so one export moves a whole stack. It exists so a staging Scufris cannot end up in the real one's conversation.

The same export moves a stack onto a forwarded socket. The mechanism built for isolation is exactly the mechanism for a remote surface, and nothing has to be added to get it.

The companion's own desktop.sock does not come along. scufris-ctl open and hud are window manager bindings for the machine the windows are on. They stay host-local, and a remote surface simply does not have them.

04

Presence

One primitive, three fixes

The argument for presence being the right primitive is that it is one concept and it closes four of the five broken rows in 02 at once. Speech stops doubling because only one surface is told to speak. Widget reports stop duplicating because only one surface was given the command. Window raises stop being noise because only the surface in front of you is asked to raise. That is not three fixes, it is one fix seen three times, which is the sign it is at the right level.

Message Goes to Why
state every surface Knowing what Scufris is doing is not exclusive
transcript every surface One conversation, seen from wherever you are
speak presence Two speakers saying one paragraph is the worst outcome here
widget presence One command must produce one report
conversation presence A window raises where you are looking
submit, abort from anywhere Already correct. Steering is defined behaviour

How presence moves. The cheapest rule that is also right most of the time: the last surface to submit holds it. You asked from the phone, so the phone answers. You typed on the laptop, so the laptop speaks and shows the panel. It needs no new client message at all, because submit already identifies its sender.

The rule is wrong in exactly one case, and it is a case the vision cares about: the machine may initiate substantive contact. Unprompted speech goes to whatever you touched last, which may be the phone in a drawer. Two things already cover it. D5 of the inversion design says the frontend synthesises what it is handed and may refuse it, so a phone can be built to never speak unasked. And an explicit claim message can be added later by a surface that knows its own foreground state, which a phone app does and an X11 window mostly does not.

So presence is staged: infer it now, let a surface assert it when there is a surface that can.

05

The catalog

The one presence does not fix

A frontend announces what widgets it has installed, the service remembers it, and it types the agent's widget tools — including for an agent that connects later, which is what makes the tools survive a debug lease. One store, last writer wins.

A phone announces nothing. It has no shelf, no window manager, no monitor corners, no Python backends and no today on its PATH. If it overwrites the store, your widget tools vanish for as long as the phone was the most recent to connect, and come back when the desktop reconnects. The agent's tool surface would flap by surface, not by capability.

Proposal: union at registration, refuse at relay. Keep a catalog per surface and hand the agent the union, so the tool surface is stable for the life of the session. Route each widget command by presence, as L6 says, and when the presence holder does not have that widget, answer the agent with the existing failure shape. Scufris tries to open a panel, hears it cannot, and says so. That is honest and it uses a road that already exists (WidgetReport::Failed with a refusal code).

Unverified: can the agent's tools change mid-session?

The union is chosen partly because it is stable, which matters only if re-typing tools on a live Pi session is difficult or impossible. That has not been checked. Before building, read the installed Pi RPC documentation for whether a tool set can be re-registered on a running session.

If it can, a third option opens: the catalog is exactly the presence holder's, and the tools honestly disappear while you are on the phone. That is a nicer model and it is cheaper to reason about. It is only unavailable if Pi says so.

06

What a phone actually is

Not the pill

"it could be just a new surface, basically just the pill as a phone app"

The pill does not port, and it is worth being precise about why, because the reason is the entire substance of the pill. It is an always-on-top X11 window, cut down to the ellipse inscribed in its own rectangle so the desktop shows around it, positioned bottom-center of a monitor, raised by a global activation hotkey, living under focus rules that exist because tao hands accept-focus back from a draw handler. A phone has no always-on-top overlay, no global hotkey and no window manager. Every one of those sentences is about a desktop.

What ports is not the pill but what the pill is a client of: four message types. So the phone surface is the conversation window plus a microphone button, which is the HUD we already built and understand, in a different frame.

Companion part On a phone
Transcript view Ports. It is the whole app
Text submit Ports
State indicator Ports as a status line, not an orb
Microphone Ports. On-device dictation, or POST to the host whisper-server over the same tunnel
Speech playback Ports, and should default to off
The pill window Does not port. It is an X11 overlay
Activation hotkey Does not port. No global keys on a phone
Widget shelf Does not port. Per-window webviews on monitor corners, fed by host processes
Tray Does not port
desktop.sock Does not port. Window manager bindings are local

Speech was already designed for this

Speaking and listening are frontend-local and never cross the service socket. There is no speaking field on the wire and the service never hears about audio. stt.rs is already a configurable HTTP endpoint.

That decision was made for a different reason - the pill, the tray and the widget clocks all live in the process that owns the speaker - and it pays for the phone entirely. Voice on a second surface costs nothing in the service.

The laptop is the cheaper case and should come first. It runs the real scufris-desktop unchanged, over a forwarded socket, with pill and widgets and all. It needs no new user interface — only the multi-frontend work in 04. The phone needs that work and a new surface and the version discipline in 07. Doing them in that order means the protocol work is proven by something you can rebuild before it is depended on by something you cannot.

07

Versions

The genuinely new constraint

The socket is not the hard part. This is. Every Scufris component today moves together, because one nixos-rebuild moves them, which is why L2 could forbid migration outright and cost nothing. Presence means a v4 protocol. Under L2, v4 means every surface changes at once.

A phone cannot promise that. Three options, and the choice is deployment taste rather than engineering:

A. Keep L2 whole

The service speaks one version. A stale app is told which version it spoke and shows one screen: your host is newer, update. Costs one panel. No compatibility matrix, ever.

B. One version of overlap

The service accepts v4 and v3 for one release. Buys an update window, costs a test matrix and a second code path in the one place that must never be wrong.

C. Additive-only for frontends

Unknown fields and unknown message types are ignored rather than refused. Cheapest to live with, hardest to hold to, and it quietly repeals L2 in the direction that matters.

Settled: A. It is a personal app you sideload onto your own phone. B and C both buy a convenience you do not need with a permanent complication in the protocol, and L2 has been earning its keep.

What A owes you in return

A is only fair if the mismatch is announced rather than discovered. Two things make it so, and they are the design work this decision creates:

  • The host tells you. On release, Scufris says "update the phone app when you can" - unprompted, in words, through the road it already has for saying things. Not an error screen you find later when you reach for it.
  • The handshake is frozen forever. A client one version behind must still be able to say that. So the hello / welcome exchange and the version refusal are permanently fixed in shape: they are the only messages that must survive every future mismatch. Everything else in the protocol stays free to change, which is what L2 is for.

The second is the one that is easy to get wrong. If the version check rides on anything that can change, the notice is the first casualty of the change it exists to announce.

08

The ladder

Four rungs, stop anywhere
  1. Today, no code. ssh den scufris-ctl watch and scufris-ctl send. Control clients are already unlimited. No pill, no voice, no widgets, but the conversation is reachable from the laptop right now, and it costs nothing to try this evening.
  2. Tunnel and forward, still no service code. A real companion on the laptop. This is where the livelock in 02 bites, so this rung is a diagnosis rather than a destination. Worth doing once, to watch it happen.
  3. Multi-frontend. Drop the eviction, add presence, decide the catalog. Protocol v4. One file plus a field in hello. This is the actual work and it is contained.
  4. The phone. A new surface: transcript, submit, state, microphone. Plus the version decision in 07, which becomes real the moment the first build leaves your machine.

Gate

None of this starts before v0.5.0 is tagged and deployed. The tree is forty-four commits ahead of the last tag with the service, the conversation window, the widget layer and the journal panels all unreleased. Adding a protocol version on top of an unshipped protocol version is how a release stops being possible.

09

To settle

Yours

D1 — Does L1 get narrowed, or does this design get refused?

Narrowing L1 to "one person, one conversation" and adding L5 and L6 is the load-bearing decision. Everything else is consequence. Refusing it is a legitimate answer: one screen is a real constraint that has kept this codebase small.

Recommended: narrow it, and add L5 in the same breath so the security clause is never quietly lost.

D2 — Version policy for a surface you cannot rebuild

Settled on revision 1: A. L2 stays whole. The host speaks one version, and a client that is behind is told so plainly.

Two obligations came with it: Scufris announces the mismatch unprompted rather than waiting to be found, and the handshake that carries the announcement is frozen in shape forever. See 07.

D3 — How presence moves

Inferred from the last submit, an explicit claim, or both in stages.

Recommended: infer now, add the claim when a phone exists to send it.

D4 — The catalog

Union at registration, or exactly the presence holder's. Blocked on the Pi question in 05.

Recommended: read the RPC documentation first, then prefer the presence holder's if Pi allows it.

D5 — Is the laptop enough?

Rung 3 gives you Scufris on the laptop with everything working. The phone is rung 4 and is most of the remaining cost. It is reasonable to stop at three and never write an app.

Recommended: decide this after living on rung 3 for a while, not now.

10

Not doing

Named so it stays out
  • Any TCP listener, under any conditions. This is L5 and it is not a trade-off to revisit.
  • Accounts, tenancy, or a second conversation. L1's surviving half.
  • Widgets on the phone. They are host processes drawing on host monitors.
  • Reachability from outside the tunnel. No public endpoint, no relay service, no push notification road. A phone with no tunnel is a phone with no Scufris, and that is the correct behaviour.
  • Session sharing in Pi. Already answered in the inversion design: RPC mode is bound to one client for its lifetime. Surfaces multiply in front of the service, never behind it.
